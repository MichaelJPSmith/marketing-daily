Greece is exploring a sovereign satellite communications system for its armed forces, and the way the partnership is forming is worth a look. On 12 October, METLEN, Thales Alenia Space and Thales Hellas signed an MoU in Athens to assess a secure national satcom capability. Thales Alenia Space would bring the satellites and ground infrastructure, METLEN gallium supply for future spacecraft, and Thales Hellas the local engineering integration. Next come joint feasibility studies, then proposals to the Hellenic Ministry of National Defence. It is linked to Greece's wider EUR 350 million Hellas-Space 2.0 national space programme. Early-stage, but the pattern is clear: governments are building sovereign space capability around work-share and local supply chains, and the companies that get in early are the ones already known to the people writing the requirement. This is exactly the kind of procurement moment commercial space companies need to be in the room for, which is exactly what Space-Comm Expo Europe exists to deliver.
