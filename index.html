Space Imaging Just Got Sharper
Tibidabo Scientific Industries acquires XCAM Scientific, adding space-qualified camera systems already flying on NASA and ESA missions.
Tibidabo Scientific Industries
UK / USA  |  Space Imaging & Optics
Acquisition
Tibidabo Scientific Industries has acquired Northampton-based XCAM Scientific, adding a team whose space-qualified camera systems have already flown on NASA's OGRE mission and the AlSat Nano CubeSat, with a Particle Fallout Monitor for ESA's Ariane 6 in the works.
🔗 Source: Business Weekly
👤 LinkedIn Post
Space imaging just got a serious boost - straight out of Northampton. Tibidabo Scientific Industries has acquired XCAM Scientific, the Northampton-based camera specialist behind flight hardware on NASA's OGRE mission and ESA's upcoming Ariane 6 Particle Fallout Monitor. XCAM's imaging systems have already flown on the AlSat Nano CubeSat and are backed by the UK Space Agency and international partners including ISRO. Space careers don't start with a rocket - they start with a challenge. Viasat has launched the first-ever Australian edition of its Space for Good Challenge, its global STEM initiative inviting university and TAFE students to design space-based solutions to real-world problems, from orbital debris mitigation to satellite connectivity for social good. Since 2024 the programme has engaged more than 10,000 students worldwide, with 88% of past participants saying it sparked interest in a space career. Product Launch
VPT has introduced the VSCPL1210SG, a point-of-load DC-DC converter built for small satellites and NASA Class D missions. It delivers up to 10A at up to 95% peak efficiency and is radiation-tested to 40krad(Si) for reliable operation in low Earth orbit.

Powering small satellites just got more efficient. VPT, Inc. has introduced the VSCPL1210SG, a point-of-load DC-DC converter built for small satellites and NASA Class D missions. It delivers up to 10A at up to 95% peak efficiency, runs from -55C to 105C with no derating, and is radiation-tested to 40krad(Si) for reliable operation in low Earth orbit.
